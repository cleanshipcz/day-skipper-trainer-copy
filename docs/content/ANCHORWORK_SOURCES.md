# Anchorwork content review basis

Reviewed 2026-08-01. The learner text is deliberately qualitative: safe anchor
choice, scope, deployment and recovery depend on the installed equipment,
vessel, seabed, conditions and local rules. Vessel and equipment manuals remain
authoritative for operating limits and procedures.

## Claim-level basis

- **Planning, selection and watch:** RYA *Safety Boat Handbook* anchoring
  guidance supports planning for conditions and bottom, controlled lowering,
  setting, checking position and maintaining a watch. The catalogue qualifies
  these principles by anchor design/size, vessel displacement and windage,
  rode, seabed, forecast load and maker instructions; it gives no universal
  type or scope rule.
- **Crew, rode, windlass and recovery hazards:** MCA *Code of Safe Working
  Practices for Merchant Seafarers* (2024), chapter 26, supports pre-operation
  communication, keeping personnel clear of moving/loaded equipment, controlled
  anchor handling, and precautions around windlass, cable and fouling. The page
  adapts those general hazard controls to small-craft language without inventing
  equipment limits.
- **Continuing watch and collision regulations:** the US Coast Guard official
  amalgamated Navigation Rules provide the International COLREG text. Rules 5
  and 7 support continuing lookout and use of all available means to assess
  collision risk; Rule 30 controls lights and shapes for vessels at anchor,
  including its size and circumstance-specific provisions. The lesson therefore
  says to apply the rule rather than presenting one display as universal.
- **Restrictions and local requirements:** charted prohibitions, harbour
  directions, protected areas and local signals vary by place and time. The
  page tells learners to obtain current local information instead of encoding a
  supposedly universal list.
