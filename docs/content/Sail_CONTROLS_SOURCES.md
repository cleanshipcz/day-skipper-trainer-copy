# Sail Controls content basis

The Sail Controls catalogue describes the depicted conventionally stayed sloop. Rig and reefing arrangements vary, so the learner-facing text identifies typical effects without presenting tuning settings as universal.

## Taxonomy

- Halyards, sheets, outhaul, Cunningham and reef controls are movable control lines: **running rigging**. The depicted vang/kicking strap is an adjustable tackle and the depicted topping lift is an adjustable line, so these catalogue entries also belong in running rigging. Rigid vangs and fixed topping lifts exist, but are outside the scope of these two diagram entries.
- The mainsheet traveller and adjustable jib fairlead/car are **deck hardware** that position a line's lead or attachment point; they are not themselves lines.
- The backstay supports the mast and is **standing rigging**; only its adjuster is operated as a trim control.

## Authoritative source basis

- [Seldén line management](https://support.seldenmast.com/en/products/line_management.html) identifies halyards, sheets, Cunningham, topping lift, outhaul, kicking strap and reef controls as lines, and distinguishes fairleads and sheet leads as hardware.
- [UK Sailmakers: mainsail trim](https://www.uksailmakers.com/encyclopedia-5-1-mainsail-trim/) distinguishes adding luff tension with the halyard, a boom downhaul and a Cunningham; it also describes outhaul, mainsheet and vang effects on foot and leech tension.
- [North Sails: how to reef a mainsail](https://www.northsails.com/en-it/blogs/north-sails-blog/how-to-reef-a-mainsail) describes single- and double-line variations, requires load at the reef tack and clew, and says intermediate reef ties only gather loose sail.
- [UK Sailmakers: genoa trim](https://www.uksailmakers.com/encyclopedia/genoa-trim/) describes how jib sheet tension, fore-and-aft lead position, halyard/luff tension and forestay sag interact.
- [UK Sailmakers: sail-trim tips](https://www.uksailmakers.com/2020/02/20/2020-2-20-21cfa8mw5pu7ncjgae5qzdl5tz3uu7/) explains that excessive jib trim stalls leech flow and that trim must respond to telltales and conditions.
- [North Sails: Nordic Folkboat tuning guide](https://www.northsails.com/en-uk/blogs/north-sails-blog/nordic-folkboat-tuning-guide) gives a concrete stayed-sloop example: easing the traveller leeward reduces heel, while added backstay flattens/opens the main and reduces forestay sag. The catalogue describes the traveller's direct function as moving the mainsheet attachment and boom/sail plan athwartships; it does not attribute an independent leech-opening mechanism to traveller movement.
- [North Sails: trim fundamentals](https://www.northsails.com/en-us/blogs/north-sails-blog/how-to-trim-like-a-pro) states that an open leech spills power and that sheet tension, jib-car position and backstay/forestay tension work together to control depth and twist.

These sources support short paraphrases rather than universal numeric settings. Vessel manuals and sailmaker tuning guides remain authoritative for a particular boat.
